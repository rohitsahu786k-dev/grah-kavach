In a real fire there is no time to read the instructions printed on the cylinder. Fire services around the world teach one memory aid for using a portable extinguisher: PASS. Learn it once and you will be able to act in seconds.

Before you start: should you fight the fire at all?

Use an extinguisher only if all of these are true:

  • The fire is small and has not spread beyond its starting point, such as a single pan, bin or appliance.
  • Everyone has left or is leaving the building, and the fire brigade (101) has been called or someone is calling.
  • You have a clear exit behind you and are not standing in smoke.

If any of these is not true, leave and close the door. A dry powder extinguisher of 2 kg has a brochure-listed discharge of only about 10 to 12 seconds, so it is meant for small fires.

The four steps

P: Pull the pin

Hold the extinguisher upright and pull the safety pin from the handle. This breaks the tamper seal. Do this at a safe distance while you are still looking at the fire.

A: Aim low

Point the nozzle at the base of the flames, not the top. Fire burns at the fuel, and that is where the powder needs to land. Stand about 2 to 3 metres away. The brochure-listed throw for the Graha Kavach extinguisher is roughly 3 to 4 metres.

S: Squeeze the handle

Press the handle firmly to release the powder. Release it to stop. Keep your other hand on the hose or nozzle so that it does not swing.

S: Sweep side to side

Move the spray across the base of the fire from one edge to the other. Advance slowly only if the flames are dying down. Keep going until the flames are out and then a little longer, watching for any re-ignition.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Aiming at the flames' tips. The powder passes through the flames and does nothing.
  • Standing too close. The powder can blow burning oil out of a pan. Back off to the recommended distance.
  • Standing too far. The powder spreads thin and scatters before it reaches the fire.
  • Turning your back. Never turn away from a fire you have just put out. Keep watching it for a few minutes.
  • Using it on the wrong fire. An ABC extinguisher is suitable for common household fires, but never fight a gas cylinder fire. Evacuate.

Practise without discharging

You cannot test-fire a household extinguisher without using it up, but you can still rehearse. Take it off the wall, check the pin, hold it as you would in a fire and say the four steps aloud. Do it with everyone in the family at least once.
