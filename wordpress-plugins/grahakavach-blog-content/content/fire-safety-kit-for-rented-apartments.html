If you rent your home, fire safety can feel like the landlord's job, and drilling holes feels risky for your deposit. But a fire does not care who owns the flat. Your belongings, and more importantly your family, are the ones at risk. The good news is that a solid fire safety setup is easy for renters, and it moves with you.

Start by asking the right questions

  • Does the building have fire safety systems? Ask about alarms, hydrants, sprinklers and the fire exit stairs, and where the society or landlord keeps extinguishers.
  • Is the wiring in good order? Ask when it was last inspected, and whether there is an RCCB and a proper earth connection.
  • What is the LPG arrangement? Piped gas or cylinders, and where are they stored?
  • Where are the exits? Locate the nearest staircase and check that it is not locked or blocked.

What a renter should own

You are responsible for what is in your own flat. A practical set is:

  • An ABC dry powder extinguisher near the main door.
  • A fire blanket near the kitchen for pan fires. See how to use it.
  • An automatic fire ball for rooms that sit empty, such as a spare room or storeroom. See how it works.
  • A battery-powered smoke alarm in the bedroom passage, which does not need wiring.

Mounting without damaging the wall

  • Check your lease first. Some allow a few small holes. A couple of screw holes are easy to fill with putty when you leave.
  • Use a floor stand or a hook on the door. The fire ball comes with a stand, so it needs no drilling at all.
  • Keep the blanket and extinguisher in an obvious open place such as on top of a shelf or in a basket near the door, if you do not want to mount them. Stay visible and within reach.
  • For permanent mounts, ask the landlord. Most are happy to approve a safety fitting.

Placement rules are the same as in your own home. See where to place a fire extinguisher.

Special risks for flats

  • You cannot escape quickly from upper floors. Memorise two routes out and never keep the main door's key out of reach. Keep grille keys on a hook near the door.
  • Smoke travels through shared corridors. If your door is hot or the corridor is full of smoke, stay inside, seal the door gaps with wet cloth, and call 101 with your exact floor and flat number.
  • Old wiring. Do not overload sockets. See common fire risk zones in Indian homes.

When you move

A kit is portable. Check the gauge, pack the extinguisher upright and secure so it cannot roll in transit, and fit it in the new place on day one, before the boxes are unpacked.
