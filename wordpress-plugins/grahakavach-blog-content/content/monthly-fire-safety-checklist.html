Fire safety equipment is like a spare tyre: you hope you never need it, but you want it ready. A short monthly check, ten minutes on the first day of the month, finds most problems before an emergency does. Print this list or save it on your phone.

1. Extinguisher (2 minutes)

  • Is it still in its place, visible and reachable?
  • Is the pressure gauge needle in the green zone?
  • Are the safety pin and tamper seal in place?
  • Any dents, rust, cracks in the hose or powder leaking around the nozzle?
  • Turn it upside down once and shake it gently a couple of times. This stops the powder inside from packing down.

2. Fire ball (1 minute)

  • Is it sitting steadily in its stand, away from the stove and not exposed to sun or water?
  • Is the shell free of cracks, dents and discolouration?
  • Check the brochure-listed shelf life and note the replacement date.

3. Fire blanket (1 minute)

  • Is it within easy reach of the kitchen door, with the pull tapes hanging free?
  • Is the pouch intact and free from oil and moisture?

4. Kitchen and gas (2 minutes)

  • Check the LPG hose for cracks and the clamps for tightness. Apply soapy water to the joints and look for bubbles. Never use a flame to check for a leak.
  • Switch off the regulator when you are not cooking.
  • Clear the area around the stove of cloth, paper and plastic.
  • Clean the chimney or exhaust filter, where grease collects.
  • More in our LPG safety guide.

5. Electrical (2 minutes)

  • Look for plugs, sockets or extension boards that feel warm, smell of burning, or show black marks.
  • Make sure no socket is overloaded with several heavy appliances.
  • Check the distribution board for loose or humming parts, and test the MCB and RCCB trip button if your board has one.
  • More in electrical panel fire prevention.

6. Escape routes and alarms (2 minutes)

  • Are the passages, stairs and the main door free of boxes, shoes and cycles?
  • Do grille keys hang where everyone can reach them?
  • Test your smoke alarm if you have one, and replace batteries as needed.
  • Keep 101 (fire) and 112 (emergency) written down near the phone and as saved contacts.

Once a quarter

  • Walk through your escape plan with the whole family, including children and older relatives. See fire safety planning for elderly family members.
  • Review your fire risk zones.

Keep a simple log

Note the date and any action taken: "gauge OK, hose clamp tightened, ball moved". It takes seconds and shows you what keeps recurring.
