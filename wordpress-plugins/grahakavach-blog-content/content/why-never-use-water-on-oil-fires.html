When oil in a pan catches fire, the instinct is to grab the nearest jug of water. It is also the most dangerous thing you can do. Water on a burning pan can turn a small, manageable fire into a fireball that reaches the ceiling in a second.

What actually happens when water meets burning oil

Cooking oil burns at very high temperatures, far above the boiling point of water. Water is also heavier than oil, so it sinks beneath the burning layer. There it flashes into steam almost instantly. Steam takes up many hundreds of times more space than the water did, and it throws the burning oil upward and outward in droplets.

Each droplet is burning, so the fire is suddenly spread across the stove, the wall, the cabinets and sometimes you. The result is often serious burns and a fire that is now far larger than before.

Why kitchen oil fires start

  • Oil left heating while you step away to answer the door or phone.
  • Overheating while deep-frying, especially with a thin-bottomed pan.
  • Wet food dropped into hot oil, which splatters onto the flame.
  • Loose clothing or a dupatta end touching the burner.

What to do instead: a step-by-step response

  1. Turn off the heat if you can do so safely without reaching over the flames.
  2. Smother the fire. Cover the pan with a metal lid or a baking tray, or use a fire blanket. Without air the flames die. Our guide to using a fire blanket on a kitchen pan fire shows how to hold it so your hands stay protected.
  3. Leave the lid on. Do not lift it to check. The pan stays hot enough to reignite for a long time, so let it cool completely.
  4. Use a dry powder extinguisher only if the fire is larger and you cannot cover the pan. Stand back and aim at the base. See the PASS technique.
  5. Get out and call 101 if the fire spreads beyond the pan.

What not to do

  • Do not throw water, and do not use a wet cloth or towel.
  • Do not carry the burning pan to the sink or outside. Spilled burning oil spreads the fire and burns you.
  • Do not use flour, sugar or detergent powder. Some of these can burn or even explode.
  • Do not fan the flames to blow them out.

Prevention habits

  • Never leave heating oil unattended. Switch off the stove if you have to step away.
  • Keep a lid or fire blanket within reach of the stove, not on the other side of the kitchen.
  • Dry food well before putting it in hot oil.
  • Keep long sleeves and loose cloth away from the burners.
  • Keep your home's fire risk zones in mind, and the kitchen at the top of the list.
