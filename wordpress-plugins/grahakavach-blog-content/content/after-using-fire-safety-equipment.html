Putting out a fire is only part of the job. What you do in the next hour decides whether it stays out, whether anyone is hurt by smoke or hot surfaces, and whether your equipment will work the next time. Here is a clear sequence to follow.

Immediately after the fire is out

  1. Stay and watch. Do not walk away. Many fires reignite within minutes from hot oil, smouldering fabric or a damaged wire. Keep watching the spot for at least 15 to 30 minutes.
  2. Call 101 anyway if in doubt. If the fire was more than a pan flare-up, or you cannot be sure it is completely out, ask the fire brigade to check.
  3. Ventilate. Open windows and doors to clear smoke and fumes. Dry powder can irritate the eyes and throat, so avoid breathing the dust and keep children and pets out of the room.
  4. Cut the power to any affected circuit or appliance and do not switch it back on until an electrician has inspected it.
  5. If gas was involved, close the regulator and keep the area ventilated. Do not use switches or flames.

Cleaning up

  • Dry powder: wear a mask and gloves. Vacuum or sweep it up gently, then wipe surfaces with a damp cloth. The powder is not toxic in normal quantities, but it can be corrosive over time on metal and electronics, so clean it up promptly.
  • Burnt oil or food: let the pan cool completely before moving it.
  • Smoke damage: wash walls and ceilings, and wash or discard food and fabrics that were exposed.

Your equipment after use

Fire extinguisher

Even if you used it for only a few seconds, it has lost pressure and cannot be relied on again. Have it refilled by an authorised service provider or replace it. Never hang a used extinguisher back on the wall.

Fire blanket

Treat a blanket that has covered a fire as used. Heat, oil and soot can weaken the fabric, so replace it. Do not wash and reuse it. See the fire blanket guide for correct use.

Fire ball

Once a fire ball has burst, it is spent. Remove the residue and fit a new one. Read how automatic fire balls work.

Find out why it happened

Before you move on, ask what caused the fire: overheated oil, an overloaded socket, a gas leak, a charger left on a bed. Fixing the cause matters more than the equipment. Our guides to fire risk zones in Indian homes and electrical panel safety help you spot the usual culprits.

Tell your insurer and record the incident

If there was damage, take photographs before cleaning, keep receipts, and inform your home or shop insurer as soon as possible. Note the date and cause so you can review your plan.

Get ready again

  • Replace or refill everything that was used straight away. A home without a working extinguisher is exposed.
  • Update your monthly checklist with what you learned.
  • Talk to the family about what went well and what did not.
