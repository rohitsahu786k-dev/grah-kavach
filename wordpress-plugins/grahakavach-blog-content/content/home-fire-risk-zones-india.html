Every home has a few places where a fire is most likely to start. Knowing them is half the work of preventing a fire. Here are the common risk zones in Indian homes, why they matter, and one simple fix for each.

1. The kitchen

Hot oil, open flames, gas and loose clothing in a small space make this the top risk zone.

  • Never leave heating oil unattended. See why water must never be used on oil fires.
  • Keep a fire blanket within reach of the stove.
  • Keep cloth, paper and plastic away from burners.

2. The LPG cylinder and stove area

A leaking hose or regulator can fill a kitchen with gas.

  • Switch off the regulator at night. Check the hose for cracks.
  • Never test a leak with a flame. See the LPG safety guide.

3. Switchboards and extension boards

Overloaded sockets and loose wiring are among the most common causes of household fires.

  • Avoid plugging heavy appliances into extension boards, or chaining boards.
  • Replace warm, discoloured or loose plugs and sockets.
  • Read about electrical panel fire prevention.

4. Charging points and bedrooms

Phones, laptops, power banks and e-scooter batteries charging overnight on a bed or sofa can overheat.

  • Charge on a hard, non-flammable surface, away from bedding and curtains.
  • Use the original charger and unplug it when the device is full.
  • Never charge an e-vehicle battery in the only exit route of the house.

5. Air conditioners, geysers and heaters

Long running hours, dust and old wiring raise the risk, particularly in summer and winter peaks.

  • Service ACs every year and use a dedicated, correctly rated socket.
  • Switch geysers off after use. Keep heaters a metre away from anything that can burn.

6. The pooja area

Diyas, incense and camphor are a daily part of home life and a frequent ignition source.

  • Use a stable metal or stone tray, away from curtains, garlands and cloth.
  • Never leave a lit diya unattended, and extinguish it before you sleep or leave.

7. Balconies, store rooms and under the stairs

These fill with old newspapers, paint, cardboard and chemicals. A discarded cigarette or a spark from a neighbour can set them alight.

  • Clear them out regularly and keep flammable liquids in a cool, ventilated place.
  • An automatic fire ball is useful in rooms that no one visits for days.

8. Diwali and festival season

Lights, firecrackers and decorations raise risk every year. Keep fireworks outdoors and away from buildings, use certified LED lights, and keep a bucket of sand or a blanket nearby.
