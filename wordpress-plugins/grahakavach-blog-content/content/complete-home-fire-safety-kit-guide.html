Most home fires in India start small: a pan of oil that overheats, a socket that sparks, a gas stove left on. In the first two or three minutes a small fire can still be stopped. After that it usually cannot. A home fire safety kit exists for those first minutes, and it works only if the right tools are in the right place and everyone in the house knows they are there.

The three layers of a home fire safety kit

No single tool handles every kind of fire. A good kit combines three, each with a different job.

1. Dry powder fire extinguisher (manual response)

The Graha Kavach kit includes a 2 kg ABC dry powder extinguisher. "ABC" means it is designed for three common fire classes: A (paper, wood, cloth), B (flammable liquids such as oil and petrol) and C (gas). The brochure-listed discharge time is about 10 to 12 seconds with a throw of roughly 3 to 4 metres, so you must aim correctly and use it early. Our guide on the PASS technique explains how.

2. Automatic fire ball (works when you are not there)

A fire ball is a ball filled with extinguishing powder. When flames reach it, it bursts and releases the powder over the fire. Because it needs no one to operate it, it can help in a room that is empty at night or while the family is out. Read more in how automatic fire balls work.

3. Fire blanket (smothering)

The kit's fibreglass blanket measures 1 x 1 metre and has a brochure-listed heat resistance of 550 degrees Celsius. It is the quickest answer to a small pan fire or a fire on clothing because it cuts off the oxygen without any powder or mess. See the step-by-step guide to using a fire blanket on a kitchen pan fire.

Where each item should go

  • Extinguisher: near the main exit or in a passage, not inside the kitchen where a fire could block you from reaching it. See where to place a fire extinguisher.
  • Fire blanket: on a wall just outside the kitchen door, within arm's reach, at about chest height.
  • Fire ball: on its stand in a spot with fire risk but little foot traffic, such as near the kitchen entrance or the electrical panel area. Never place it directly over a stove.

The kit ships with wall-mounting hardware and a stand, so you do not need extra fittings.

Keeping the kit ready

  • Check the extinguisher's pressure gauge every month. The needle should sit in the green zone.
  • Make sure nothing blocks access to any item, and that the blanket's pull tapes are not tangled.
  • Note the brochure-listed shelf life of each product and mark the replacement date on a calendar.
  • Use our monthly fire safety checklist to make this a habit.

Know when not to fight the fire

Equipment is for small, contained fires when you have a clear way out behind you. Leave immediately, close the door behind you and call 101 (fire) or 112 (emergency) if the fire is spreading, the room is filling with smoke, or you are unsure. Your life matters more than any property.

Make it a family plan

Show every adult and older child where the kit is and how each item works. Agree on a meeting point outside the house. A kit that only one person knows how to use is only half a kit.
