LPG is clean, convenient and used in most Indian kitchens. It is also heavier than air, so a leak settles near the floor and can wait quietly for a spark. Almost all LPG accidents are preventable with a handful of habits.

Safe installation

  • Keep the cylinder upright on a flat, dry surface, in a ventilated spot, below the level of the stove. Never lay it down or place it in a closed cabinet without ventilation holes.
  • Use a BIS-marked rubber hose (ISI mark), no longer than about 1.5 metres, without kinks, and clamp it at both ends. Replace it before it cracks. Many suppliers recommend every couple of years.
  • Keep the hose away from the hot side of the stove and from sharp edges.
  • Keep the cylinder away from heaters, the flame, direct sunlight and electrical switches.

Daily habits

  • Switch off the regulator at night and whenever you leave the house, not only the stove knob.
  • Never leave a cooking flame unattended.
  • Light the match or lighter before turning on the gas, not after.
  • Keep the stove surround clean of grease and cloth.
  • Do not let children play near the stove or the cylinder.

How to check for a leak

  1. With the stove off, apply soapy water to the hose, clamps and regulator joint.
  2. Open the regulator. If bubbles form, there is a leak.
  3. Switch off the regulator and get a mechanic or your distributor to fix it. Never test with a flame.

If you smell gas

LPG has a strong added odour, similar to rotten eggs, so that leaks can be noticed. Act at once:

  1. Do not light a flame, switch any electrical appliance, lights or fan on or off, or use a phone in the room.
  2. Close the regulator if it is safe to reach.
  3. Open all doors and windows to let the gas disperse.
  4. Move everyone outside.
  5. Call your gas distributor's emergency number or 1906 (LPG emergency) from outside, and 101 if there is any fire.
  6. Do not go back in until you are told it is safe.

If there is a fire at the stove or cylinder

  • Small flame at the hose or stove: if you can safely reach it, close the regulator. The flame will die as the supply stops. A fire blanket can smother a small flame at the burner.
  • A pan fire next to the stove: see why you must never use water on oil fires and how to use a fire blanket.
  • Fire at the cylinder itself, or the hose burning near it: do not try to fight it. Leave, take everyone out, close the door and call 101. A heated cylinder can burst.

Be ready

Keep an extinguisher and a fire blanket just outside the kitchen. See where to place your extinguisher, and make gas checks part of your monthly checklist.
