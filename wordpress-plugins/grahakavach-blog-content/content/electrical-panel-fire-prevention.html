The electrical distribution board, called the DB box, MCB box or panel, is the heart of the wiring in any flat or shop. Every circuit passes through it, so a fault here can start a fire that spreads quickly behind walls. Most panel fires are preventable.

What causes panel fires?

  • Loose connections. A loose terminal creates resistance, which makes heat, which loosens it further.
  • Overload. Too many appliances on one circuit, or an MCB of too high a rating for the wire.
  • Poor quality or old components, and DIY repairs.
  • Moisture and dust, especially in the monsoon. See monsoon electrical fire safety.
  • Pests. Rats and insects gnawing wire insulation.
  • Blocked ventilation and storage in front of the board.

Warning signs

  • A burning or hot plastic smell near the board.
  • A hot panel cover, or warm MCBs.
  • Buzzing, humming or crackling.
  • Brown or black marks and melted plastic.
  • An MCB that trips repeatedly, or lights that flicker across the house.

If you notice any of these, switch off the main supply if safe to do so, and call a licensed electrician. Do not keep resetting a breaker that keeps tripping.

Prevention checklist

  1. Get an annual inspection by a licensed electrician, including tightening of terminals and thermal checks if available.
  2. Use the right MCBs and an RCCB. Ratings must match the wire size and the load. An RCCB cuts power on earth leakage and protects people as well as property.
  3. Label each circuit, so you can switch off the right one in an emergency.
  4. Keep the area around the panel clear. Leave at least a metre free. No boxes, clothes, paper or paint.
  5. Keep the cover closed and fit blank plates over any open slots.
  6. Do not overload. Spread heavy loads like AC, geyser and induction across separate circuits.
  7. Never repair it yourself or bridge a tripping breaker.

If a panel catches fire

  1. If you can safely reach the main switch from a dry position, turn it off. If not, do not approach.
  2. Never use water.
  3. For a small fire, stand at a distance and use a dry powder extinguisher, aiming at the base. See the PASS technique.
  4. If the fire is spreading, the panel is inside a wall or smoke is building up, leave and call 101.
  5. Do not switch the power back on until an electrician has checked the board.

Equipment placement

Keep the extinguisher near an exit, a few steps from the panel, so that you never have to stand directly in front of a burning board to reach it. A fire ball placed nearby, but not touching the panel, adds protection when the premises are empty. See where to place a fire extinguisher.

Apartments and shops

In apartments, check whether the society has its own common panels and who maintains them. In shops, panels are often hidden behind stock, so keep the way to the panel free at all times. See the small shop fire safety checklist.
