An extinguisher only helps if you can reach it in seconds. Too many are tucked away in a cupboard, hidden behind a door or mounted inside the very room that is burning. Where you put it matters almost as much as owning one.

The five rules of placement

  1. Near an exit. You should be able to grab it on your way out, with the door at your back. If the fire grows, you are already at your escape route.
  2. Visible and unobstructed. Keep it on a wall or in the open, never behind furniture, curtains or stored boxes. Everyone in the house should know where it is.
  3. At a reachable height. Mount it so the handle sits roughly 1 to 1.2 metres from the floor, so adults can lift it off easily while children cannot play with it.
  4. Away from heat sources. Do not mount it directly above a stove, near a heater, or in direct sun. Heat and temperature swings affect the contents and pressure.
  5. Close to risk, but not inside it. Put it a few steps outside the kitchen rather than next to the stove. If the stove is burning, you will not be able to get to something mounted beside it.

Room-by-room guide

Kitchen

Keep the extinguisher in the passage or hall just outside the kitchen door. Place a fire blanket next to it, since a blanket is the first choice for a pan fire.

Living room and hallway

A hallway near the main door is the best single spot in many flats. It is on the escape route and is reachable from most rooms.

Bedrooms

Fires from chargers, heaters and AC units often start at night. If you have a multi-storey home, keep one on each floor near the staircase.

Near the electrical panel

Keep it a short distance away rather than directly in front of the board, so you do not have to stand in front of a burning panel. See electrical panel fire prevention.

Shops and offices

Place one near the entrance and another near the stock or wiring area. See the small shop checklist.

Spots to avoid

  • Inside a locked cupboard or storeroom.
  • Above or beside the gas stove.
  • On the balcony in open sun or rain.
  • Behind doors that swing open onto it.
  • On the floor where it can be knocked or tripped over.

Mounting tips

  • Use the bracket supplied and fix it to a solid wall with proper screws. Brick or concrete is best.
  • Hang it so that the pressure gauge and safety pin face outwards and are easy to see.
  • Renting? Look at fire safety for rented apartments for low-damage mounting options.

Check it regularly

Look at the pressure gauge monthly. The needle should be in the green zone. Check that the pin and seal are in place and that nothing is blocking access. Our monthly checklist turns this into a 10-minute routine.
