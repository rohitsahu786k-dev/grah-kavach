A small shop or office holds a lot in a small space: stock, packaging, wiring, and often years of savings. It is also empty for many hours a day, which means a small fire can grow unnoticed. A few practical steps can cut that risk sharply.

Know your shop's biggest risks

  • Stock and packaging. Cardboard, plastic, cloth, paper and foam burn quickly.
  • Overloaded wiring. Extension boards, multiple plugs, AC units, lights and billing equipment on one circuit.
  • Heat sources. Tea or cooking arrangements, heaters, irons or soldering tools.
  • Closing time. Equipment left on overnight, with nobody present.
  • Blocked exits. Stock stacked in front of the shutter or back door.

Good housekeeping

  • Keep stock off the floor and at least a few feet from lights, panels and heaters.
  • Keep a clear path at least one metre wide from the counter to the exit, and never block the shutter or back door.
  • Store flammable items such as thinners, perfumes and cleaning liquids in a cool place away from stock and electricals.
  • Clear the dust and cobwebs from fans, lights and panel boxes.
  • Empty bins at closing time. Do not leave paper and packaging stacked in a corner.

Electrical discipline

  • Do not daisy-chain extension boards, and use only good quality boards with a switch and fuse.
  • Have the wiring checked by a licensed electrician at least once a year.
  • Unplug chargers, heaters and non-essential equipment at closing time.
  • Learn more in electrical panel fire prevention.

Closing-time routine

  1. Switch off appliances at the plug, not just the button.
  2. Switch off the main supply if it is safe to do so, apart from essentials like CCTV or a fridge.
  3. Walk around the shop and check for anything warm or smelling of burning.
  4. Check the extinguisher gauge once a week and use our monthly checklist.
  5. Lock up, and save the fire brigade (101) and nearby shops' numbers.

Train your staff

Everyone who works in the shop should know where the extinguisher is and how to use it. Rehearse the PASS technique once, and agree on one meeting point outside. If a fire is not clearly small, everyone leaves first and calls 101.
