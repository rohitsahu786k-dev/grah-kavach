The rains bring relief from the heat, and a quieter rise in electrical fire risk. Damp walls, water seeping into wiring, humid air that corrodes contacts, and ACs and inverters running hard all raise the chance of a short circuit. A little preparation before and during the monsoon keeps homes and shops safer.

Why the monsoon raises electrical risk

  • Moisture in wiring. Water or damp can lower insulation resistance, leading to leakage current, sparking and tripping.
  • Corrosion. Humid air corrodes terminals and plug pins, which raises resistance and heat at the connection.
  • Seepage. Leaking roofs, terraces and AC drain lines can send water into switchboards, conduits and ceiling lights.
  • Heavy use. Inverters, UPS units, dehumidifiers, geysers and ACs run longer during storms and power cuts.

Before the rains

  1. Inspect for damp. Look at walls and ceilings around switchboards, fans and lights for stains or peeling paint. Fix the leak first, then call an electrician.
  2. Test earth and RCCB. Make sure your home has an earth connection and that the RCCB (residual current breaker) trips when you press its test button.
  3. Seal outdoor points. Use weatherproof covers for outdoor sockets, meters, lights and AC outdoor units.
  4. Replace damaged wiring. Brittle, cracked or patched wires are an invitation for a fault.
  5. Service the AC and inverter, including battery terminals and drain pipes.

During the monsoon

  • Never touch switches, plugs or appliances with wet hands or while standing on a wet floor.
  • If a switchboard is damp, wet or sparking, switch off the main supply from a dry position (use a dry wooden stick or wear rubber-soled footwear if needed) and call an electrician. Do not use it until it is fully dry and checked.
  • Avoid extension boards on the floor. Keep them above flood and splash level.
  • Do not overload a single socket with an iron, heater and geyser at once.
  • Unplug appliances you are not using during thunderstorms.
  • If water enters the house, switch off the mains before the water reaches sockets.

Warning signs

  • A burning smell near a socket or switch.
  • Warm or discoloured plates and plugs.
  • Crackling or buzzing sounds.
  • MCB tripping repeatedly, flickering lights or mild shocks from metal fittings.

Treat any of these as urgent. Switch off that circuit and get it inspected.

If an electrical fire starts

  1. Switch off the power at the main switch if you can do it safely.
  2. Never use water on a fire near live electrics.
  3. For a small fire with the power off, use a dry powder extinguisher. See the PASS technique.
  4. If it spreads or you are unsure, leave and call 101.

Keep the right gear nearby

Keep an extinguisher near the exit and a fire ball near the distribution board, but not directly in front of it. See electrical panel fire prevention and where to place an extinguisher.
